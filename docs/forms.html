Forms

Forms and the pieces that go in them: fields in a row, options under a choice, choice rows, a question card, matching, sorting and a card sort.

Page Setup and Forms App

Put cmu-app on <body> on pages with the CMU header and footer, or cmu-framed on pages shown inside another tool (such as a Canvas LTI frame) without them. cmu-app also styles forms: a label in small gray capitals over each text box or menu, which fills its column; a legend in the same style over each group of checkboxes or radio buttons, listed without bullets, with each label beside its box; and room under each. On any page with the kit, checkboxes, radio buttons, sliders and progress bars are CMU red. A p.cmu-hint right after a field (or after a fieldset's list) sits just under it, with the room below moved under the hint; point the field's aria-describedby at it. Add inline to a text box or menu to keep its natural width. Group checkboxes in a fieldset so the legend names them for screen readers. A legend that asks a question, as in Choice Rows, Matching or Sorting, stays bold and in sentence case.

Fields in a Row App

Short fields that belong together on one line, each with its label above: a width and a height, a date range, a first and last name. Wrap each label and its field in a div inside cmu-field-row; a separator such as × or "to" goes between them in a span with aria-hidden="true", since the labels already say what each field is. A hint under the row works as under a single field. When the row is too narrow, the fields wrap onto the next line. For short values, such as numbers or codes, add cmu-field-row--short so the boxes keep a short width instead of sharing the whole row. To name the pair, such as Size for a width and a height, put the row in a fieldset with a <legend>: the legend takes the small capitals, and the fields’ own labels turn quieter, in ordinary case.

Options Under a Choice App

Options that only apply to one choice go inside that choice's list item, lined up with its label, in a cmu-sub-options block. The kit shows them only while that choice is picked, with no script: put the radio button or checkbox right inside the list item, before its label. Menus and text boxes inside fill the block, as they fill a column elsewhere. Try it: pick A Logo.

Choice Rows App

A question with a few answers, each a large row that's one target: the radio button or checkbox and its words, with the box around it, so it's easy to pick on a phone. Put each <input> inside its <label>, and the question in the fieldset's <legend>. The picked row turns red. Use checkboxes the same way when more than one answer can be picked. A question in the legend is bold; when the legend only names the choices, such as Pages under a heading that already asks, add cmu-label for the small capitals of other labels.

Question Card App

One question at a time, such as in a training, a quiz or a poll: a white card on a colored band, with the question over a rule and its choice rows. The card is cmu-question on the form; the band is the template's content wide with a color, here red. Checking the answer, and what happens next, is up to the app.

  • Under the choices, cmu-question-buttons puts a forward arrow ( ▶ ) on the right and, when people can go back, a back arrow ( ◀ ) on the left (cmu-question-back).
  • On the last question, the forward arrow gives way to Submit, centered, so someone clicking forward doesn't submit by accident.
  • Give each arrow an aria-label and a matching title, "Previous question" and "Next question," as with the arrows in Sorting.

Matching App

Items to pair with answers, such as terms and what they mean, drawn as puzzle pieces: each item has a socket, and the menu of answers beside it a tab. Once an answer is picked, the two pieces snap together. It's a menu, not dragging, so it works with a keyboard, a screen reader and a phone. Each menu starts on "Choose," with an empty value, and has a <label> naming its item. Under 600px the menu goes under its item, without the puzzle shapes.

Sorting AppScript

Items to put in order, such as the steps of a task: a numbered list with up and down arrow buttons on each item, which cmu-app.js adds. Each button names its item for screen readers, focus stays on the item as it moves and its new place is read out. Put a hidden <input> in each item; it moves with the item, so the form sends the new order. Without the script, the list keeps its first order.

Card Sort App

Things to sort into groups, such as uses of AI into public and protected tools: each thing is a white card, with a large tile for each group at its bottom. Cards sit side by side, as many as fit, and one above another on phones. Picking a tile turns it red. Each card is a <fieldset> whose <legend> holds its words, with a radio button in each tile, so keyboards and screen readers work as with any radio buttons. Put the cards on a colored band, as with the question card.
